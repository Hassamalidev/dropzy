AirDrop is great when everyone in the room owns Apple gear. The moment an Android phone or a Windows laptop joins in, it stops being an option. Android and Windows have their own nearby-sharing features, and they're useful, but they don't all talk to each other, and each device needs the right settings switched on.

Dropzy takes a different approach: it lives in the browser. Any device with a modern browser can join, whether that's Android, Windows, macOS, iPhone, iPad, Linux or a Chromebook.

## What "like AirDrop" means here

The things people like about AirDrop are simple:

- Nearby devices just show up.
- You tap one and the file arrives.
- Nothing lingers in a cloud account afterwards.

[Wi-Fi Share](/) does the same for any mix of devices on one network. Open the page and every device on that Wi-Fi appears with a friendly name, like "Blue Fox" or "Red Panda". Send something and it shows up on the other screens.

It isn't identical to AirDrop. AirDrop can use Bluetooth and a direct Wi-Fi link even with no network around. Dropzy needs both devices online in a browser. In return, it works across every platform and even across different networks.

## Sending from Android to Windows

1. Open Dropzy on the Windows PC. Leave the tab open.
2. Open Dropzy on the Android phone in Chrome, Firefox, Samsung Internet or another browser.
3. Tap **Choose files** and pick photos, videos, PDFs, APKs, anything.
4. The files appear on the PC. Click to download, or use **Download all** for a ZIP.

From Windows to Android is the same in reverse. You can also drag files onto the page, or paste a screenshot straight from the clipboard with Ctrl+V.

## Mixed groups: the Room

AirDrop shines when you're passing photos around a group. For that, use a [Room](/room/new):

1. One person creates the room.
2. Everyone else joins with the 6-digit code, the link or the QR code on [the Join page](/join).
3. Anyone can add files, and anyone can remove them.
4. When everyone's in, lock the room so no one new can join.

A room lasts 24 hours and can be extended to 48. It's a neat fit for a family weekend where half the phones are Android and half are iPhones.

## When devices are on different networks

AirDrop only works with devices that are physically close. Dropzy's [Private Share](/private) doesn't have that limit. It creates a private link and QR code for you and one other person, or for your own devices, wherever they are. Your phone can be on mobile data and your laptop on the office Wi-Fi.

Private Share is end-to-end encrypted. The key is inside the link, so the server never sees file names or contents. It lasts 2 hours and you can extend it to 6. You can also share a single file with its own link.

## Big files

When the other device is online, files go **directly** from one browser to the other. They aren't uploaded, and there's no fixed size limit beyond the free space on the receiving device. iPhone and iPad browsers cap this at about 1 GB.

If a direct connection can't be made, Dropzy uploads the file for a few hours instead, up to 2 GB per file, then deletes it automatically. You don't have to pick; it falls back by itself.

## Troubleshooting

### A device is missing from the list

Some things make devices invisible to each other on the same Wi-Fi: a VPN, iCloud Private Relay on an iPhone, guest networks that isolate devices, or a phone that slipped onto mobile data. Use the **Connect a device** card. Scan the QR code, or enter the 6-digit pair code on [Join](/join).

### You're on public Wi-Fi

On very busy networks, Dropzy hides other people's items. Find yours by its 4-character code, or use Private Share for a clean one-to-one connection.

### An iPhone photo won't open on Windows

iPhones save photos as HEIC by default and may convert them to JPEG or keep them as HEIC when sending. Windows can need the HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store to open HEIC. Details are in [the iPhone to Windows guide](/guides/iphone-photos-to-windows-pc).

## Built-in sharing vs. Dropzy

Your phone's own nearby-sharing feature is a fine choice when both devices support it and it's already set up. Dropzy is handy when:

- the devices are from different makers,
- you're using a work PC where you can't install things,
- you're helping someone who doesn't want to change settings, or
- the devices aren't in the same place.

Both can live happily on the same phone.

## Privacy

No account, no tracking cookies, and nothing is kept for long. Wi-Fi Share items last 2 hours. Direct transfers never touch a server, and uploaded files are deleted automatically. See [How it works](/how-it-works) for a plain-English explanation.
